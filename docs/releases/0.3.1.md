## A faster phone app that looks like a chat app

**It's quicker.** The phone used to rebuild the whole screen two or three times for every few
milliseconds a finger moved, slid sheets in at 20 frames a second, and copied every contact and the whole
open conversation on every frame. Now each frame handles all the touches since the last one in a single
pass, anything that slides (a sheet, a swiped list) runs at the display's own rate, and the screen's data is
only rebuilt when something actually changed.

**The home screen is your conversations.** A rail down the left holds Messages, your group chats and
communities, adding someone and your invite. Beside it is **Messages**: search, an add button, then one row
per conversation with their picture, the last message and when it was. You sit in a bar along the bottom:
your name, whether your door is open (with the switch), and Settings. With nobody added yet, the same screen
shows the two ways to start.

**Chats read like a chat app.** Messages run down the left under each person's picture, name and time.
Tap a message for its tools, including the new **Reply**: the reply carries a small quote of what it
answers. Web links get a card under the message; tap it to open the link in your browser. Nothing is fetched
from the site, so no one learns who is talking to whom.

**Profiles.** Tap yourself in the bottom bar for your profile: banner in your colour, picture, name,
pronouns, status and About me, with **Edit Profile** to change them all on one screen. Tap a contact's
name at the top of their chat to see theirs, with Message and Call buttons. Pronouns are new, and go to the
people you call along with your picture and About line.

Replies and pronouns are understood by older versions too: they just show the message without the quote,
and the profile without pronouns. The desktop shows pronouns now; replies there come in a later release.
